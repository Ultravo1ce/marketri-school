How to learn marquetry from scratch: where a beginner should start

Tatiana Gorina School of Marquetry, 9 November 2026

Marquetry may seem like a craft for the chosen few, but any patient person can master it. Here is how the path from your first design to your own panel works.

What you need to start

Woodworking experience and drawing skills are not required. For your first works you are given a ready-made sketch, and a steady hand comes with practice. Other things matter much more:

  • patience — a design is made of dozens and hundreds of pieces;
  • attention to detail — a gap of half a millimetre shows on a finished work;
  • interest in the material — in time you will start to see colour, lustre and figure in veneer, the way a painter sees paints.

From simple to complex

Learning marquetry usually takes three steps. This is exactly how our course is built.

  1. A simple ornament. A geometric pattern in two or three species: squares, diamonds, a star. It teaches you to hold the knife, cut along a line and fit pieces together without gaps.
  2. A picture from a sketch. A landscape, a flower or a still life in many species. This is where choosing veneer by colour and grain and sand shading come in.
  3. Your own work. Your own sketch, gluing onto a backing board, sanding and finishing. The result is a finished panel or a furniture inset.

Typical beginner mistakes

A dull blade. It tears the fibres and leaves a ragged edge. Change the blade as soon as it starts to drag the veneer instead of cutting it.

Cutting in one pass. Veneer is cut in several light passes. Heavy pressure breaks thin pieces and pulls the knife off the line.

Ignoring the grain. Veneer splits easily along the grain. Thin pieces are cut so that the grain runs along their long side.

Weak pressure when gluing. Without a press or clamps the design lifts in bubbles. Glue under even pressure and let the glue dry completely.

We describe what you will need for the work in more detail in the article “Marquetry tools”.

On your own or with a master

You can learn from books and videos, but it will take a long time. Most mistakes only show on the finished work, when it is too late to fix them. A master spots them at the cutting stage and shows techniques that are hard to grasp from a description: how to hold the knife, how to choose veneer, how to shade a piece.

Another advantage of studying in a studio is the materials. Good veneer of rare species is expensive and sold in bundles. On the course you work from the studio’s stock and try dozens of species without buying them.
