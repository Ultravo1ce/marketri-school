Marquetry tools: what you need to get started

Tatiana Gorina School of Marquetry, 9 November 2026

You don’t need a big workshop for marquetry: your first works can be made at an ordinary table. Here are the tools you will need and how to choose them.

Cutting tools

Cutting knife. The main tool of a marquetry maker. A scalpel or a knife with replaceable thin blades will do. The knife should sit comfortably in your hand and the blade should always be sharp, so you need spare blades from day one.

Fretsaw. Used for packet cutting, when several sheets of veneer are cut at once. Professionals use a chevalet, a special saw station with a horizontally mounted blade. To begin with, a hand fretsaw with fine blades is enough.

Cutting mat. A self-healing mat or a sheet of thick cardboard protects both the table and the blade.

Metal ruler — for straight cuts in geometric designs.

Transferring the sketch and assembly

  • Tracing paper and carbon paper — to transfer the drawing onto the veneer.
  • Paper tape — gummed or masking tape. It holds the pieces of the design together on the face side.
  • Tweezers — for small pieces.
  • Soft pencil — for marking.

Gluing

Backing board. The design is glued onto plywood or MDF. To keep the board from warping, veneer is glued to the back as well.

Glue. To begin with, PVA wood glue will do. It is applied in a thin, even layer.

Press or clamps. The design must dry under even pressure. Small works are clamped between flat boards or weighed down with a stack of books.

Shading

For sand shading you need clean fine sand, a metal pan and a hotplate. The edge of a piece is dipped into the hot sand and the wood darkens, creating volume and shadow.

Finishing

  • Sandpaper in several grits, from medium to fine.
  • Oil, wax or varnish — they protect the surface and bring out the colour of the wood.

A beginner’s kit

For your first works you only need: a knife and spare blades, a cutting mat, a ruler, tracing paper, paper tape, a few sheets of veneer in different species, a plywood backing board, PVA glue, clamps and sandpaper. The fretsaw and shading can come later.

If you study on our course, you don’t need to buy anything: everything is in the studio. And when you decide to work at home, the master will help you choose your tools.

Safety

Cut away from yourself and keep your other hand behind the blade. Put the knife in its sheath when you are not working. Ventilate the room when sanding or varnishing.
