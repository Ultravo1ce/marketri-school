How to care for marquetry

Tatiana Gorina School of Marquetry, 9 November 2026

A marquetry piece can last for decades if it is kept in the right conditions. Here is how to look after veneer panels, furniture and boxes.

Light

The main enemy of marquetry is direct sunlight. Under ultraviolet light wood changes colour: light species yellow, dark and coloured ones fade, cherry darkens. Because of this, the contrast between the pieces gradually disappears.

Hang panels on a wall out of direct sunlight, and keep inlaid furniture away from windows or protect it with curtains.

Humidity and temperature

Wood “breathes”: it absorbs and releases moisture. With sharp changes, veneer can warp and the pieces of the design can separate.

  • Keep the humidity in the room at around 45–60%. In winter, when the heating is on, a humidifier helps.
  • Do not place pieces next to radiators, fireplaces or air conditioners.
  • Do not keep them in damp places: on a balcony, in a basement or in a bathroom.

Everyday cleaning

Wipe the surface with a soft dry microfibre or flannel cloth. It is best to dust regularly: dust acts as a fine abrasive.

Do not use:

  • wet cloths and sponges — water can get into the joints of the design;
  • abrasive powders and stiff brushes;
  • products containing alcohol, solvents or ammonia — they damage varnish;
  • polishes with silicone — they leave a film that makes it hard to renew the finish.

Inlaid furniture

Tabletops and the tops of chests of drawers suffer from heat and moisture. Put dishes on coasters and blot spills straight away. Do not put flowerpots on the surface without a saucer.

Renewing the finish

Pieces finished with oil or wax dull over time. Every year or two the finish can be renewed: apply a thin layer of the same oil or wax and buff it with a soft cloth. Varnished surfaces only need wiping, and badly worn varnish is best left to a master.

If a problem appears

If a piece comes loose or a bubble or crack appears, do not glue it with household glue. Keep the piece of veneer that fell out and contact a master: restoring marquetry requires matching the material and careful work.
