Shading veneer with hot sand: how to create volume and shadow

Tatiana Gorina School of Marquetry, 9 November 2026

Sand shading is one of the main secrets of volume in marquetry. The piece is not painted: its edge darkens from the heat, and a flat petal becomes three-dimensional.

How it works

At high temperature the surface of wood darkens, like the crust of bread. Hot sand heats the piece gently and evenly, so the transition from the dark edge to the light centre is smooth, like shading in a pencil drawing.

The technique is several centuries old: furniture makers were already using it in the 18th century. Tatiana Gorina also darkens veneer with hot sand and teaches it to her students. You can read about her approach on the master’s page.

What you will need

  • Fine clean sand — sifted, free of clay and pebbles.
  • A metal pan — a frying pan or saucepan with a thick base.
  • A hotplate with adjustable heat.
  • Tweezers — to hold the piece without burning yourself.
  • Offcuts of the same veneer — for testing.

How to shade

  1. Pour a layer of sand a few centimetres deep and heat it on the hotplate.
  2. Test the temperature on an offcut: it should darken noticeably within a few seconds without charring or smoking.
  3. Pick up the piece with tweezers and dip into the sand the edge that should turn dark.
  4. After a few seconds, take it out and check the result. If it needs to be darker, dip it again. Several short dips are better than one long one.
  5. To darken an area in the middle of a piece, you can spoon hot sand onto it.

The deeper and longer the piece stays in the sand, the darker and wider the shadow. The sand is cooler near the surface and hotter at the bottom, which is also used to control the tone.

Where to use it

Shading brings petals and leaves to life: a dark band at the base or along the central vein instantly gives volume. It is used to emphasise folds of fabric, the roundness of fruit and the shadow at the edge of an object. In geometric patterns with cubes, shaded faces create the illusion of a third dimension.

Common mistakes

Sand that is too hot. The piece chars, becomes brittle and warps. Start with moderate heat and always test on an offcut.

Too long. Light species such as maple, birch and sycamore darken especially quickly. Count the seconds and keep the piece in only briefly.

Shading after fitting. Heat can make a piece shrink slightly. After shading, check it in place and trim it if needed.

Safety

The sand and the pan get very hot. Work with tweezers, never leave the hotplate unattended, put the hot pan on a stand and ventilate the room. Let the pieces cool after the sand before you pick them up.
