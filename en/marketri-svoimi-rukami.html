Marquetry by hand: your first work step by step

Tatiana Gorina School of Marquetry, 9 November 2026

You can make your first marquetry work at home in a few evenings. Here is how to go all the way from a sketch to a finished panel without spoiling the material.

What you will need

A sharp knife with replaceable blades, a cutting mat, a ruler, tracing paper, paper tape, three or four sheets of veneer in different species, a plywood backing board, PVA glue, clamps and sandpaper. We have put together a detailed list in the article “Marquetry tools”.

Step 1. Choose a simple sketch

For a first work, choose a design of 5–15 large pieces with clear outlines: a leaf, a flower of a few petals, a geometric ornament, the silhouette of a bird. A convenient size is about 15 by 20 centimetres. Leave fine lines and small details for later: they are hard to cut without experience.

Transfer the sketch onto tracing paper and number the pieces. That way it is easier not to get lost when you start choosing the veneer.

Step 2. Choose the veneer

Take three or four species that differ clearly in tone. Light wood such as maple or birch is usually chosen for the background, and oak, cherry or walnut for the details. Neighbouring pieces should contrast: squint at the laid-out pieces and see whether they merge.

Pay attention to the grain direction. For a leaf, run the grain along the central vein; for sky or water, run it horizontally. We explain how different species behave in the article on veneer species.

Step 3. Cut with the window method

This is the most convenient method for beginners. You work directly in the sheet of background veneer:

  1. Transfer the outline of the first piece onto the background sheet and carefully cut it out. This gives you a “window”.
  2. Slide the veneer for the piece under the window and choose the most attractive part of its figure.
  3. Score around the edge of the window with a sharp knife, leaving a mark on the sheet below, and cut the piece out along that mark.
  4. Fit the piece into the window and secure it with paper tape on the face side.

Repeat for each piece. Each piece is cut exactly to its window, so there are almost no gaps.

Step 4. Cut the right way

Hold the knife perpendicular to the sheet and cut in several light passes rather than one heavy one. Start each cut at a sharp corner and work towards the middle of the line. Veneer splits easily along the grain, so guide the knife especially carefully there. Change the blade as soon as it starts to drag the fibres.

Step 5. Glue the design onto a backing board

Spread a thin, even layer of PVA glue on the plywood and lay the design tape side up. Cover it with a sheet of paper and a flat board, then clamp everything or put it under a press. Let the glue dry, preferably for at least a day. To keep the board from warping, glue a sheet of veneer to the back as well.

Step 6. Sand and finish

Remove the paper tape and sand the surface along the grain, moving gradually from medium to fine paper. Remove the dust and apply oil, wax or varnish in two or three thin coats. The finish protects the work and brings out the colour of the wood.

If you want it faster and without mistakes

A first work is easier under a master’s eye: she will show you how to hold the knife and help you fix a mistake while it can still be fixed. On the marquetry course you go through this whole path in the first stage, and all materials and tools are in the studio.
